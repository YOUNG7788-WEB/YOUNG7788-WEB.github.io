AI Playbook

A careful briefing workflow

2026-11-14 · 1 min read

Check sources and dates, and keep unresolved items unresolved.

On this page ＋
Start reading

I've made news and business briefings as well as personal ones based on read-only checks of my calendar and inbox. The easiest mistake is overstating a status. A renewal email doesn't show that a payment went through. An empty calendar result doesn't guarantee an empty day. If I can't reach an inbox, I need to say so.

I want a briefing to help me forget fewer things. Sources, times and actions matter more than fancy formatting. Private emails and invoices stay private. The method is what belongs on this site.

I treat the two kinds of briefing separately. A personal one tells me what might need attention today: an appointment, unread email, payment deadline or person to reply to. A news briefing organizes stories and business information so I can skim for leads. Both need sources and dates. An engaging news item won't tell me whether my own bill was paid.

If an inbox is unavailable, I make “couldn't check this time” easy to see. If the email says a bill is coming due, I leave it at that. The most important step comes afterward, when I open the original email, calendar entry or account and take care of the actual task. The briefing points me toward it.
